Set Notation Reference
{ } — Curly brackets make a set ∈ — "is an element of" (is in the set) ∪ — Union: "OR" — everything in either set (or both) ∩ — Intersection: "AND" — only what's in both sets A' — Complement: everything NOT in A n(A) — Number of elements in set A ∅ — Empty set (nothing in it) U — Universal set (everything we're talking about)

n(A') = 5

n(P ∪ Q) = 7

False — "a" is not in set Q

7 In a class of 40 students:
• 22 students study French
• 18 students study German
• 8 students study both French and German
(a) Draw a Venn diagram to represent this information. [3]
Draw rectangle (U = 40), two overlapping circles (F and G). F only: 14; overlap: 8; G only: 10; outside: 8.
(b) How many students study French only? [1]
22 - 8 = 14 students
(c) How many students study neither French nor German? [1]
40 - (14 + 8 + 10) = 8 students
a Venn Diagram:
Rectangle = U (40 students total)
Circle F (French) and Circle G (German) overlapping
Overlap (both): 8
F only: 22 - 8 = 14
G only: 18 - 8 = 10
Outside circles (neither): 40 - 14 - 8 - 10 = 8
b 14 students study French only (22 total French minus 8 who study both)
c 8 students study neither: 40 - 14 - 8 - 10 = 8

9 In a survey of 50 people:
• 30 like Tea
• 25 like Coffee
• 10 like neither Tea nor Coffee
(a) Draw a Venn diagram to show this information. [3]
Draw rectangle (U = 50), two overlapping circles (T and C). T only: 15; overlap: 15; C only: 10; outside: 10.
(b) How many people like both Tea and Coffee? [1]
n(T ∩ C) = 15
(c) How many people like Tea only? [1]
30 - 15 = 15 people
a Step-by-step:
People who like Tea OR Coffee = 50 - 10 = 40
Using the formula: n(T ∪ C) = n(T) + n(C) - n(T ∩ C)
40 = 30 + 25 - n(T ∩ C)
n(T ∩ C) = 55 - 40 = 15

So: T only = 30 - 15 = 15; Overlap = 15; C only = 25 - 15 = 10; Outside = 10
b 15 people like both Tea and Coffee
c 15 people like Tea only (30 - 15 overlap = 15)
